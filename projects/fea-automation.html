Assembly modeling · CAE workflow04

Parametric finite element modeling for assembly simulation

MY CONTRIBUTION

Developed and documented the HyperWorks assembly-modeling workflow, covering CAD organization, mesh representations, batch meshing, contact and connector setup, and part-revision management. Assessed the workflow through a stringer-thickness change and identified the manual steps needed to maintain the assembly model.

PROBLEM
During the design phase, multiple parts within an assembly may undergo geometry or dimension changes. Each revision can require updates to the assembly FE model, including remeshing affected components, redefining contacts, and adapting connections between neighboring parts. Repeating these operations manually creates redundant work and increases the risk of inconsistencies. The challenge is to develop a parametric FEM workflow that reuses modeling definitions and supports targeted, consistent updates as the design evolves.
METHOD
Organized the CATProduct assembly in the HyperWorks Part Browser and grouped skins, frames, stringers, and cleats into part sets. Assigned mesh representations, generated Thin Solid meshes with a 4 mm element-size setting, and defined assembly interactions using automatic contact detection and point connectors. Managed CAD changes through major revisions stored in the part library, retaining part identifiers and including affected neighboring components.

From CAD assembly to finite element representations

Assembly interactions

Auto Contact creation

Used the Auto Contact tool to detect neighboring surfaces and generate assembly interfaces from selected components, proximity tolerance, and contact type. Defined a contact pair between the two skins and tie contacts for the frame, stringer, and cleat interfaces.

Connector creation

Defined a point-type bushing control, then created and realized connectors between the two skins. Each connection combines two RBE2 couplings and a CONN3D2 element to represent the assembly connection.

View Part Browser and contact-setup screenshots

Automation with engineering control

Tool-assisted: batch meshing from assigned representations, automatic contact detection, and connector realization from predefined controls.

Engineer-led: selecting the revised and affected parts, defining suitable settings, checking mesh quality, and regenerating contacts or couplings where geometry changes require it.

RESULT
Demonstrated a targeted update of the assembly FE model following a stringer-thickness change. Reused the part-library and representation workflow to retain the original configuration as Major Revision A, introduce the revised stringer and attached cleats as Revision B, and generate their updated meshes. The example shows how revision tracking and reusable modeling definitions support local design updates without starting the assembly-model preparation from scratch.

The demonstration assembly comprises 2 skins, 3 frames, 7 stringers, and 24 cleats.

Design-change example: stringer thickness

A thickness change in Stringer 7 also changes the position of its attached cleats. The revision therefore covers the affected group of parts, rather than the stringer alone.
