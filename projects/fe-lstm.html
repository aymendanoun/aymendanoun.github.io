FE-LSTM: a hybrid model combining Artificial Intelligence and FE analysisto accelerate multiscale simulations of architected materials

MY CONTRIBUTION

Developed the FE-LSTM approach, trained the neural networks using TensorFlow, and implemented their integration into the FE solver. Assessed macroscopic stress accuracy, execution time, and memory usage against FE² benchmarks.

PROBLEM
Capturing the nonlinear behavior of heterogeneous and architected materials through multiscale simulation requires significant computational time and resources.
METHOD
Developed FE-LSTM, a hybrid approach combining finite element analysis with LSTM neural networks trained on offline microscale simulations. The networks predict the history-dependent homogenized material response, replacing repeated microscopic FE calculations while retaining the macroscopic FE framework.

What makes FE-LSTM different?

FE-LSTM replaces the repeated microscale simulations required by FE² with fast predictions from a trained LSTM network. The macroscopic finite element model continues to enforce structural equilibrium, reducing computational cost while accounting for the microstructure’s nonlinear response.

Why use LSTM neural networks?

Nonlinear material responses can depend on loading history. LSTM networks retain information from previous increments to predict how the homogenized stress response evolves.

RESULT
Reduced computational time by up to 99%, enabling demanding multiscale simulations to run on standard desktop computers without requiring HPC resources.

FE-LSTM / COMPARISON WITH FE²

FE-LSTM closely matches the FE² stress and strain fields, capturing stress concentrations around the holes while substantially reducing computational cost.
