Torquebox door-surround structure

MY CONTRIBUTION

I contributed to the stress analysis and sizing of the Torquebox Door Surround Structure (DSS) for an aerospace development program: preparing and integrating detailed finite element models, assessing critical load cases, computing reserve factors, and evaluating laminate-thickness changes to reduce mass while satisfying the applicable sizing criteria.

01 / PROJECT & CONCEPT

Rethinking the structure around a door opening.

The Torquebox is an omega-shaped Door Surround Structure (DSS) made from thermoplastic composite, developed to transfer fuselage loads around passenger or cargo door openings. Its omega-shaped frames, lintel and sill form an integrated surround. Compared with the conventional Ladder arrangement of separate structural members and numerous metallic junctions, the concept aims to maintain load-path continuity and create smoother transitions around the door corners.

Weight-saving potential. Better load distribution can reduce corner stress concentrations, the skin reinforcement needed around the opening, and the number of titanium fasteners.

Lower part count and assembly effort. Integrating structural functions reduces the number of parts and junctions to manufacture and assemble, limiting drilling, shimming and fastener installation. This also creates opportunities to improve material utilization and shorten assembly lead time.

Industrial opportunities. Automated preform preparation and press forming of thermoplastic parts support the objective of high production rates. Welding and co-consolidation offer further integration opportunities, while the reference concept retains conventional bolted assembly.

REFERENCE / LADDER

Ladder Door Surround Structure

ALTERNATIVE / TORQUEBOX

Torquebox Door Surround Structure

02 / ANALYSIS PROCESS

From concept CAD to a sized structure.

The workflow connects detailed local modeling to the global fuselage response, then uses the most demanding reserve factors to guide the laminate updates.

  1. 01

    Concept CAD

    Define the Torquebox geometry and the interfaces with the surrounding fuselage structure.

    Geometry & part interfaces
  2. 02

    Detailed mesh / DFEM

    Mesh the component group: skin, frames, Torquebox, sill and intercostals. Represent the laminate areas and structural connections.

    Detailed finite element model
  3. 03

    Integration into the GFEM

    Connect the detailed model to the global fuselage model, including model condensation where required, to represent the aircraft load paths and boundary conditions.

    Integrated global–detailed model
  4. 04

    Stress analysis

    Run the applicable load cases and recover element forces, strains, stresses and joint loads. Identify the critical areas of each component.

    Critical locations & load envelopes
  5. 05

    Reserve-factor assessment

    Compute RFs for the applicable composite sizing criteria and find the controlling criterion for each part or laminate area.

    RF maps & governing criteria
  6. 06

    Laminate optimization

    Add plies where RF < 1; investigate ply removal where margins are high. Recheck every applicable criterion after each update.

    Revised laminate & mass balance

03 / MY CONTRIBUTION: STRESS ANALYSIS

Identify the criterion that governs each area.

Recovered FE results are assessed using the relevant laminate properties, local geometry, loading and environmental conditions. Each applicable criterion produces its own RF; the lowest relevant RF governs the local sizing decision.

04 / OPTIMIZATION LOOP

Reduce excess thickness. Preserve the sizing margins.

RF < 1

Reinforce

The assessed criterion is not satisfied. Add composite plies or revise the local design, then recompute the RFs.

RF CLOSE TO 1, ≥ 1

Target the useful margin

Seek an efficient laminate while satisfying every applicable criterion and the stacking and manufacturing constraints.

HIGH RF

Investigate ply removal

Screen a thinner laminate, then verify that another criterion or load case does not become limiting.

The target is to bring the governing RFs as close to 1 as practical, while remaining at or above the required threshold.

05 / ENGINEERING OUTCOME

Supporting a lighter structural concept.

The Torquebox concept achieved an overall 10% mass reduction compared with the Ladder reference. My contribution supported structural assessment and laminate optimization while satisfying the applicable sizing criteria.

Integrating structural functions also offers opportunities to reduce part count and simplify assembly. Detailed masses, component breakdowns and assembly counts are omitted to preserve project confidentiality.
