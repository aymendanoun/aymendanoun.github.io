Electrochemical process simulation · Aerospace02

Anodization simulation of an aircraft cockpit slice

MY CONTRIBUTION

Delivered the complete simulation workflow: model setup, meshing, implementation of nonlinear electrochemical boundary conditions, solving, and post-processing of potential and current-density fields.

PROBLEM
The anodization process forms a protective oxide layer that improves corrosion and wear resistance. Predicting oxide-thickness distribution on complex aircraft structures requires a finite element model that captures nonlinear electrochemical reactions at the metal–electrolyte interface and applies appropriate current-density and potential boundary conditions. The challenge is to resolve these interactions to assess layer uniformity and support process optimization and quality control.
METHOD
Developed a steady-state finite element model of the anodization process using Elmer’s Static Current Conduction solver. Represented the electrochemical response at the metal–electrolyte interface through nonlinear current-density boundary conditions fitted to experimental polarization data at 25°C. Applied the model to a reduced industrial aircraft cockpit-slice geometry to analyze potential and current-density distributions.

Model assumptions and validation
  • Steady-state current conduction with constant electrolyte conductivity.
  • Cathode potential of −10 V; nonlinear oxide response imposed on the anode surfaces.
  • Current-density/potential-drop relationship fitted with a third-degree polynomial at 25°C.
  • COMSOL comparison showed good agreement in potential gradients, oxide potential-drop distribution and anode current density, with a reported validation range of 97–104 A/m².

Potential differences and gradients were compared rather than absolute potential values, because the two implementations use different reference-potential conventions.

RESULT
Applied the simulation workflow to an aircraft cockpit-slice geometry, identifying local current densities of approximately 62–106 A/m². Higher values occurred near the cathodes, while recessed surfaces showed lower values, revealing non-uniform treatment conditions. These calculated current-density variations identify regions at risk of uneven oxide-layer growth and provide a basis for evaluating cathode placement and treatment uniformity. Local oxide thickness could be estimated from the current density using Faraday’s law for a specified treatment time.
